# Six-week supervised pilot

## Decision to make

Can three nearby pantries and one coordinator reliably close real category shortages with existing surplus, at less staff and transport cost than their current process?

**Status:** proposed. No pantry has agreed to participate.

## Before the pilot

Confirm a network operator, approved locations and transfer permissions. Agree who can accept, drive and receive. Document food safety rules, actual pickup/receiving windows, transport equipment and incident ownership. Use only food the operator confirms may be transferred. Disable public demo mode, use HTTPS and persistent storage, test restore, and establish an account recovery owner.

Conduct five short discovery interviews. Ask for a recent specific transfer, its category/quantity, calls made, outcome and time spent. Ask about transfers abandoned and why. Observe the real spreadsheet or forms with permission. Do not ask for recipient names or income.

## Week 1: baseline

Use the existing process. Log category gaps before service, offered surplus, feasibility, minutes coordinating, miles driven, pounds dispatched, pounds accepted, and exceptions. Separate inaccessible transport, storage constraints and insufficient supply. Avoid judging staff performance.

## Weeks 2-5: run and learn

Each participating site enters a short next-service category request and only surplus lots it wishes to share. The coordinator reviews proposals. Receiving pantry acceptance happens before pickup. Driver records condition and required temperatures. Receiver records actual weight, any rejection and reason. Review each exception the same day. Compare coordinator time per completed transfer with baseline, accounting for training and entry time.

## Week 6: evaluate with operators

| Measure | Definition | Illustrative success threshold to agree before starting |
|---|---|---|
| Actual received pounds | Sum of receiver-accepted weight once per transfer | Report actual total; no arbitrary pounds quota |
| Category gap closed | Actual received pounds against each declared service-category need | At least 20% reduction against baseline in participating service windows |
| Coordination time | Entry + review + calls + receipt time per completed transfer | At least 25% reduction from baseline |
| Receipt completeness | Completed physical transfers with full receiving record | At least 95% |
| Safety exceptions | Rejections, missing temperature logs, policy violations | Review all; pause on an unresolved incident |
| Adoption | Sites entering current needs/surplus each agreed cycle | At least 3 sites consistently participate |
| Willingness to pay | Budget-owner decision on explicit paid offer | One credible paid continuation or documented rejection reason |

These targets are proposed, not validated benchmarks. A small uncontrolled pilot cannot establish causal changes in household food insecurity. No meals or households helped are inferred from pounds moved.

## Interview script

1. Tell me about the last time a category ran short before a service.
2. Was suitable food available elsewhere? How did you find out?
3. Who could authorize its movement, and what would stop it?
4. How long did coordination and recordkeeping take?
5. Which food must never appear in a transfer suggestion?
6. What information do you already enter elsewhere?
7. Who would fund a tool that solved this, and what would it need to prove?
